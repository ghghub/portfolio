Your product needs to be understood

When you find your competition out in the market selling at half the quality you offer, but you do not know how to get across what your product is worth, you usually try to explain yourself in more detail to make yourself understood. What you actually need is for your client to have a real, deep understanding of your technical information, translated into terms they can grasp. The truth is you will not be able to stand at every point of the circuit explaining everything. That wears anyone down, and you know your knowledge and leadership are needed in other areas of your company. How do you take back control and start making the decisions that matter for your business?

What happens when the market can finally read what you do.

In many cases, the different points your product has to pass through before reaching its end consumer are staffed by people who do not have the clarity your production team has. Sometimes your own team also gets lost among the different proposals, and that usually creates problems when it comes to delivering what they do best.

One of the challenges that hurts most in a technical company is when the product (or service) is compared on uneven terms: it holds a large amount of technical knowledge built up through experience and track record, and that knowledge still does not weigh on the buying decision of whoever decides. There is value in the successive implementations and adjustments in quality, materials and embedded technology, but none of it shows up in the offer, and it means your qualified staff spend a lot of time explaining it.

At this turning point it is time to consolidate the effort: time and market exposure should have given your company specialised knowledge that makes the difference. What is missing is integrating it and scaling the impact it generates.

By this stage many companies realise their products deliver several solutions, carry embedded technology and follow regulated processes, and that all of it stays invisible in a tender or at an industry event. In some cases the company has grown and is entering new markets, but does not yet have there the reputation it does have among its long-standing clients.

If this is your case, it is a key moment to strengthen your offer, presenting the best of your company clearly and understandably, so you can reach the kind of project you are able to take on. Aligning perception and capability.

When your business is aligned and explains itself, you stop competing on price.

Your technical team gets time back for what matters.

And the projects that reach you are the ones you can take on.
